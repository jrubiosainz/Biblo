Clicked a phishing link? Here's what to do

It happens to careful people too: in September's simulation, 18 % of us clicked. What matters is the next ten minutes. Nobody gets into trouble for clicking; the only mistake is to keep quiet.

In the next ten minutes

  • 1
    Report it. Select the email and use the Report button in Outlook, then Report phishing. It reaches us, and we can block it for everyone.Don't forward it to colleagues to ask what they think, and don't delete it before you report it.
  • 2
    Typed your password? Change it now, from the usual sign-in page, never from a link in an email.Then tell the IT desk, so they can check your recent sign-ins.
  • 3
    Call the IT desk: extension 3300, 24 hours a day. Say what you clicked, when, and what you typed.
  • 4
    Opened an attachment? Take the laptop off the Wi-Fi and the cable, leave it on, and call. Leaving it on helps us see what happened.
  • 5
    A sign-in request you didn't start? If your authenticator app asks you to approve a sign-in you didn't make, deny it and call: someone may already have your password.
  • 6
    Gave card or bank details? Call your bank too, and block the card.

The three signs, before you click

  • 1
    It rushes you. “Your account will be locked in 24 hours.” If an email hurries or threatens you, slow down.
  • 2
    The sender almost matches. Read the whole address: contoso-soporte.co is not contoso.com.
  • 3
    The link asks you to sign in. Hover over it before you click, and never type your password on a page you reached from an email.
